Just a Casual Sunday. (It Was Not Casual.)

by Kelsey · 5 min read · filed under: plant mom, dog mom, certified chaos

I had one goal yesterday. One. Build a plant table, maybe get some staining done, call it a day. A productive Sunday. A focused Sunday. A Sunday where I was a person with a plan.

The plan immediately involved Dutch Bros and an Australian Shepherd Blue Heeler mix with a God-given mission to supervise everything and everyone within a half mile radius, so you can already see how this went.

First Stop: Dutch Bros, Obviously

Every great day starts with caffeine and Powder knows this. He was in the car before I even had my keys. Powder is an Aussie Blue Heeler boy — wired, intense, deeply convinced that monitoring the situation is always his responsibility — and at our Dutch Bros he has somehow charmed every single broista into treating him like a regular. They know his name. They talk to him before they talk to me. I ordered my drink and watched three different people lean out the window to say good morning to my dog like he was holding court, which, honestly, he was.

He accepted the attention with the focused energy of someone who was already thinking three stops ahead. Classic Powder.

Home Depot: A Herding Emergency

Here's the thing about building a plant table from scratch — you need actual lumber. Real wood. From a store. Which means loading a very alert Australian Shepherd Blue Heeler into the car and heading to Home Depot with a dream, some rough measurements, and absolutely zero formal woodworking experience.

Powder had thoughts about Home Depot. Many thoughts. Urgent thoughts.

First, he barked at a random employee. Not aggressively — just one sharp, decisive bark, the kind that says I see you and I want you to know that I see you. The employee thought it was hilarious. I apologized. Powder did not apologize because he did not feel he had done anything wrong, which, from a herding dog's perspective, he hadn't.

Then he found a man shopping for lumber and decided this man needed to be supervised. Immediately and thoroughly. He planted himself next to this complete stranger and watched, with absolute intensity, as the guy sorted through boards — tracking every movement, every board picked up, every board put back. The man kept looking at me. I kept shrugging. Powder kept his eyes locked on the lumber situation like it was his personal responsibility that this stranger made good choices.

At one point the guy said "he's really into this" and I said "he's herding you" and the guy laughed but I want to be clear — I was not joking. That is exactly what was happening. Powder was herding a man in the lumber aisle of Home Depot on a Sunday morning and he was extremely good at it.

We got the lumber. Powder accepted pats from approximately six strangers on the way out, each one earned, none of them requested. A successful mission.

The Part Where I Actually Built a Table

Back home, I laid everything out in the yard and stared at it for a while. I had a vision — a proper plant table, something with levels, something that said yes, I am a person who takes their plants seriously and also built this with their own hands from real wood they selected themselves while their dog supervised a stranger. I had no blueprint. I had vibes, a saw, a can of walnut stain, and a dog who immediately began circling the lumber pile like he was assessing the perimeter.

The staining came first. Each board by hand, warm walnut, slow and meditative — until it isn't meditative anymore and it's just your back hurting and Powder trying to herd the stain brush. I redirected him approximately forty times. He remained convinced the brush needed watching.

Then the actual building. Measuring, cutting, figuring out angles that my brain kept overcomplicating. There were a few moments of sitting on the floor staring at a board thinking okay but which end. There was one stripped screw. There was a point where Powder stationed himself directly in the center of my workspace and I had to work around him because moving a Blue Heeler who has decided he is in the correct location is simply not an option.

But then — it came together. Levels and everything. Real wood, real stain, built from scratch in my yard on a Sunday by a person who started the morning at Dutch Bros with her dog and a loose idea.

I stood back and looked at it and felt genuinely, unreasonably proud. Powder sniffed the finished table once, confirmed it met his standards, and walked away. High praise.

The Philodendron Incident (Bonus Chaos)

While moving all my plants to their gorgeous new home, I noticed my philodendron giving me the look. Plant people know the look. Yellow at the base, sad drooping leaves, soil that had clearly been overwatered at some point by someone who shall remain nameless. It was me. It was absolutely me.

Root rot. Classic. Devastating. Fixable, but only if you act fast. So I pulled the whole thing out, trimmed the sad roots, dusted with cinnamon — yes, cinnamon, it's antifungal, look it up — repotted in fresh well-draining mix, and gave her a full pep talk out loud while Powder sat beside me and stared at the plant with the focused concern of a dog who is not sure what the problem is but is prepared to help if herding becomes necessary.

She's going to be fine. I've decided this and it's final. She's going to thrive on the table I built and we don't need to discuss the twenty minutes I spent anxiously Googling root rot recovery timelines after Powder was asleep.

Powder's Official Assessment

Powder would like it known that he was present and alert for every stage of this project and considers his contributions essential. The lumber was sorted correctly. The workspace perimeter was secure. The philodendron situation was monitored closely. He worked a full shift and then slept for eleven hours, which he earned.

He would also like it known that wood stain smells suspicious and someone should really be keeping a closer eye on it.

10/10 Sunday. The plant table is magnificent. The philodendron is going to make it. Powder is already mentally planning his next Home Depot visit.

The laundry is still not done.
